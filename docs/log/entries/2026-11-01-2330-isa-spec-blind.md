# ISA.md as a spec: two blind encoders, and the compiler without the DDK's container

docs/CLEANROOM.md splits the work into a spec side and an implementation side.
The Mesa-side instruction encoder is to be rewritten from docs/ISA.md by a
session that has never seen `tools/isa/hw.py` or the decompiles. ISA.md has to
be enough for that, and the only honest test is to try it.

## The test

`tools/isa/blind.py gen` draws one random valid instruction per form (174
forms; `pck` cut to 10-12 of its 64). Each must decode with no unexplained
bit and re-encode from its text. Fresh sessions got only the instruction texts
and were allowed to read nothing but `docs/`. They worked out the 64-bit words
and listed every bit they had to guess. `blind.py score` compared their words
with the key.

- **Round 1** (four sessions, 122 instructions): 120 exact. The two misses were
  fsubflr's operand slots and `xsyncend`, which is h12 alone (the encoder sets
  h23 with it, the text names them separately). Only 41% of the words were
  marked sure, though. The rest rested on facts the doc implied but never
  stated. The same ones came back from all four:
  - the bank codes as numbers (the doc gave lists in order);
  - the layout inside an indexed operand;
  - which operands shrink to 6 bits under format control (r, o, pa, sa and i
    only);
  - text defaults (`mask` is 1 in some classes, 15 in sopwm, 0 elsewhere);
  - per-form operand slots.
- These went into a new "Encoding conventions" section at the top of ISA.md,
  plus per-class fixes (smp's mask/ret overlap, emit's immediate split, the
  MOE `.r` forms, ima32's hi/cin codes, `setl`/`savl`, `op0xf3`).
- **Round 2** (two new sessions, a new seed, 120 instructions): 120 exact,
  88% marked sure. Their remaining notes were small and are fixed too.

`tools/isa/isadoc_cov.py` is the mechanical half. Every form's identifying
bits, field positions, operand slots and enum values must appear in its
class's section: 4188 checks, 21 left, all written in prose that the regex
can't parse.

## The compiler stops writing USPs

The DDK's USP container and glslcompiler's numbering pins exist only for the
A/B harness. Both are now outside the compiler.
- The compiler publishes the program's structure in `struct sgx_shader`:
  blocks, samples with their smp templates, branches and SA slots.
- It takes a `struct sgx_layout` from its caller for uniform, attribute and
  sampler numbers.
- `tools/usc/sgx_usp.c` builds the USP from the struct, and sgxc keeps the
  `-u/-a/-s` tables.
- The gallium driver reads the fields instead of parsing a USP.

Across every corpus and test set, F32 and F16, the 6841 output files are
byte-identical before and after. The driver builds for x86_64; it hasn't run on
hardware yet (the EAs are offline).
